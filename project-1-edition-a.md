# MP Materials Corp. — Company Research Report

## Executive summary

MP Materials Corp. operates the Mountain Pass rare-earth mine and processing facility in California and the Independence metal, alloy, and magnet manufacturing facility in Texas. The business is organized into Materials and Magnetics segments. In 2025, Materials primarily sold neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) oxide and metal and also sold rare-earth concentrate, while Magnetics began selling magnetic precursor products to General Motors. Finished neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) magnet manufacturing began in December 2025. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, PDF pp. 7 and 11–13; filed February 26, 2026.**

MP ended 2025 with $1.166 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $664.3 million of short-term investments, or $1.830 billion combined. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, PDF p. 124; filed February 26, 2026.** The company generated $224.4 million of revenue, recorded a $149.4 million operating loss and an $85.9 million net loss, used $155.8 million of cash in operations, and reported negative free cash flow of $303.9 million in 2025. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF p. 88, and Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, PDF p. 126; filed February 26, 2026.**

## What the company sells

MP’s production chain runs from mined ore to rare-earth concentrate, separated rare-earth products, NdPr metal and alloy, and ultimately finished NdFeB permanent magnets. The Materials segment contains the upstream mining and midstream separation operations at Mountain Pass; the Magnetics segment contains downstream metal, alloy, and magnet manufacturing at Independence. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, PDF pp. 9–13; filed February 26, 2026.**

Magnetics generated $66.9 million in its first year of revenue, while finished-magnet manufacturing began only in December 2025. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 76 and 78; filed February 26, 2026.**

## What changed in 2025, according to management

### Product mix shifted away from concentrate

MP ceased all product sales to China in July 2025 to align with its U.S. Department of War agreements and domestic supply-chain objectives. The company continued producing concentrate but began prioritizing further processing and stockpiling. Management said this decision caused a 21% decline in Materials-segment revenue. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 78 and 81; filed February 26, 2026.**

At the same time, NdPr production rose 101% to a record 2,599 metric tons and NdPr sales volume rose 75% to 1,994 metric tons. Management attributed the increase in NdPr oxide and metal revenue to higher sales volumes from the continuing separated-products ramp and higher realized prices. Management also acknowledged that separated-product throughput remained below design capacity. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 78 and 96; filed February 26, 2026.**

Total revenue increased 10% because higher NdPr oxide and metal revenue and the start of magnetic precursor-product revenue more than offset lower concentrate revenue following the China exit. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 88–89; filed February 26, 2026.**

### Magnetics revenue and manufacturing began

The Magnetics segment began selling magnetic precursor products to GM in the first quarter of 2025 and generated $66.9 million of revenue for the year. MP began manufacturing finished NdFeB permanent magnets at Independence in December 2025. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, PDF pp. 7 and 13, and Item 7, PDF p. 78; filed February 26, 2026.**

### Department of War agreements and financing

In July 2025, MP entered a public-private partnership with the U.S. Department of War. The agreements include an NdPr price floor of $110 per kilogram through December 2035, an offtake agreement for the proposed 10X magnet facility, and a guarantee that the 10X facility will generate at least $140 million of annual EBITDA after reaching the specified production milestone, subject to the agreement’s definitions and escalation terms. MP also committed to use up to $600 million of existing cash for specified expansion projects. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 78–79; filed February 26, 2026.**

MP recognized $51.0 million of price-protection-agreement income in the fourth quarter of 2025. Management said this income had a significant effect on Materials results and helped produce positive Materials Segment Adjusted EBITDA. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 89–90 and 97–98; filed February 26, 2026.**

Financing associated with the year’s developments included $724.2 million of net proceeds from a common-stock offering, $400.0 million from Series A convertible preferred stock issued to the Department of War, and a $150.0 million Samarium Project Loan. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, PDF pp. 79 and 81; filed February 26, 2026.**

## Capital structure

At December 31, 2025, long-term-debt principal was $1.080 billion and its carrying amount was $998.7 million, comprising $67.4 million classified as current and $931.3 million as non-current. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Note 10, PDF p. 167, and Consolidated Balance Sheets, PDF p. 124; filed February 26, 2026.** Series A cumulative perpetual convertible preferred stock had a $413.5 million liquidation preference and a $413.6 million carrying amount. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, PDF p. 124; filed February 26, 2026.**

The reported 2025 diluted weighted-average share count was 170.1 million. Because MP reported a loss, another 66.5 million potentially dilutive shares were excluded as antidilutive, including securities related to the 2030 Notes, Series A preferred stock, the Department of War warrant, RSUs, and PSUs. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Note 20, PDF p. 197; filed February 26, 2026.**

## Risks disclosed by the company

### Execution and capital intensity

MP warns that rare-earth production is capital intensive and that continued growth depends on reaching anticipated separation rates at Mountain Pass. The company also faces construction, commissioning, qualification, and production risks as it expands Independence, develops heavy-rare-earth capability, and constructs 10X. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, PDF pp. 32–39; filed February 26, 2026.**

### Dependence on government arrangements

MP warns that funding and support under the Department of War agreements could be modified, challenged, or impaired and that the agreements contain affirmative and negative covenants that may restrict actions management considers important. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, PDF pp. 28–30; filed February 26, 2026.**

### Commodity, China, and demand exposure

MP warns that demand and prices for rare-earth and magnet materials are volatile and influenced by global supply and demand, trade restrictions, tariffs, exchange rates, downstream demand, and Chinese production and export policies. The Department of War price arrangement moderates but does not eliminate that exposure, and MP must share part of the NdPr price upside after 10X reaches full production. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, PDF pp. 26–28; filed February 26, 2026.**

### Customer concentration

Two Materials customers accounted for 33% and 25% of 2025 revenue, and one Magnetics customer accounted for 31%. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Note 2, PDF p. 132; filed February 26, 2026.**

### Operational, financing, and environmental risk

The company identifies risks including power and water disruption, equipment and processing problems, inability to meet customer specifications, reserve-estimation uncertainty, raw-material constraints, transportation disruption, labor matters, cybersecurity incidents, and environmental obligations. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, PDF pp. 24–25 and 38–53; filed February 26, 2026.** MP also warns that servicing debt requires significant cash, that operating cash flow may be insufficient, and that convertible securities may dilute common shareholders. **Source: MP Materials Corp., 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, PDF pp. 54–55; filed February 26, 2026.**
